Storm Debris and Brush Hauling After Wind or Ice

Storm debris and brush removal after wind or ice in Central Kentucky: what a yard crew can haul, what needs a tree pro, and how to prep for a cleanup visit.

Get a Free Quote Call (859) 248-0576

Short answer: After a wind or ice storm in Central Kentucky, a yard crew with a trailer can pick up downed limbs, brush, sticks, and leaves the crew can safely handle, then haul it off the property. Leaning trees, limbs tangled in power lines, and debris on or inside structures are a different job. Leave those alone and call the utility, a tree professional, or your insurer first.

What counts as storm debris vs. a scheduled cleanup?

Storm debris is what a single weather event leaves behind: snapped limbs, scattered branches, piles of twigs and leaves blown into corners, and broken shrubs. It shows up all at once, often in places that block driveways, walks, and lawns.

A scheduled cleanup is different. Spring and fall cleanups are planned resets: leaves out of beds, edges tidied, the yard prepared for the season. Storm cleanup is reactive. The goal is to make the property safe and usable again, then get the material off-site.

Lawn Busterz lists debris cleanup, including storm cleanup, leaf removal, and brush hauling, among its services, and the crew brings the trailer. That's the core of a storm visit: gather it, load it, haul it.

What a yard crew can handle

Typical storm debris jobs include:

  • Downed limbs and branches lying on the lawn, driveway, or beds.
  • Brush piles from broken shrubs or small trees that are already on the ground.
  • Sticks and twigs scattered across the yard.
  • Leaves and small debris blown into fence lines, beds, and corners.
  • Hauling all of the above off the property instead of leaving piles at the curb.

The crew loads material that can be handled safely by hand or with equipment the crew already uses. For bigger material, the skid steer can help move and load debris, and the excavator can dig out stumps and roots when a job calls for it.

What should stay put until a professional sees it

Some storm damage is dangerous. Don't attempt to move it yourself, and don't ask a yard crew to:

  • Lines down. If a limb is touching or tangled in a power line, stay away and call the utility. Treat every downed line as live.
  • Leaning or hung-up trees. A tree that's partially fallen, leaning on another tree, or has a broken limb hanging overhead needs a tree professional.
  • Debris on a roof or structure. Limbs on a house, garage, shed, or vehicle can shift unexpectedly when moved. Document the damage and contact your insurer and the right trade.
  • Damage inside the house. Water coming in through a damaged roof or window is a restoration and roofing matter.

Once hazards are cleared by the right people, a yard crew can come in for the ground-level cleanup and hauling.

Can limbs and brush go on the same visit as a mow?

Small sticks and light debris are often picked up before a mow so the mower doesn't throw them. But a yard full of storm limbs and brush is a separate job that takes more time, more labor, and a trailer. Mowing over a lawn littered with branches risks damage to equipment, flying debris, and a bad cut.

The usual order after a storm is to clear the debris first, then return to the normal mowing schedule. If you're a regular mowing customer, ask whether a storm cleanup visit can be scheduled right before your next mow.

How hauling brush differs from mowing over sticks

A mower can handle a few small twigs. It can't handle limbs, piles of brush, or branches buried in leaf litter. Hauling means the material actually leaves your property, so you aren't stuck with a brush pile slowly breaking down in the corner of the yard.

If you'd prefer to keep some material, such as firewood-sized logs, say so ahead of time so the crew sets it aside instead of loading it.

When the job is really a tree crew or an excavator

Storm cleanup sometimes reveals bigger problems:

  • A standing tree that's cracked or split needs a tree professional to assess and remove it. Lawn Busterz does not advertise tree climbing or emergency tree removal.
  • A stump left after a tree comes down can be dug out with an excavator. Lawn Busterz lists excavator work including stump removal. Note that stump grinding isn't on the published menu; excavator removal pulls the stump and leaves a hole to fill and grade.
  • Ruts and torn-up turf from heavy equipment or fallen trunks may need grading afterward, which the skid steer handles.

Knowing which trade does which part saves time when everyone is busy after a storm.

Before the crew arrives

To make a storm cleanup visit go smoothly:

  • Make sure hazards have been handled by the utility or tree professional.
  • Take photos of the damage for your records and insurer.
  • Clear a path for a truck and trailer, and unlock gates.
  • Flag anything to keep, like logs, stones, or garden items.
  • Point out buried items near work areas, such as irrigation heads, landscape lighting, or septic components.
  • Tell the crew about soft ground. After heavy rain, parts of the yard may be too wet for equipment without leaving ruts.
