Grading and Gravel Driveway Prep with a Skid Steer

Skid steer grading for gravel driveways in Central Kentucky: fixing ruts, potholes, and washouts, building a crown, moving water off the drive, and site prep.

A skid steer is the right machine for most gravel driveway repairs: cutting down ruts and high spots, filling potholes and washouts, regrading the surface with a crown so water sheds off the sides, and spreading and leveling new gravel. The job lasts only if water is moved off and away from the drive.

Lawn Busterz offers skid steer work for grading, leveling, gravel, demolition, and hauling, with the operator included. This page explains what a skid steer can fix on a gravel drive, why driveways fail again, what to flag before the machine arrives, and how grading differs from drainage trenching.

What driveway problems a skid steer fixes

Gravel driveways across Central Kentucky tend to fail in the same handful of ways:

  • Ruts. Tire tracks worn into the surface, often deepened by wet weather.
  • Potholes. Low spots where water sits, softens the base, and gets pounded out by traffic.
  • Washboarding. Rippled ridges across the drive from traffic and loose gravel.
  • Washouts. Gravel carried off by runoff, often on slopes or where water crosses the drive.
  • Soft spots. Areas where the base has turned to mud.
  • Lost crown. A flat or dished surface that holds water instead of shedding it.

A skid steer with the right attachment can cut down high spots, pull gravel back from the edges, fill and compact low areas, rebuild a crown, and spread fresh stone evenly. For many drives, regrading the existing material plus a top layer of new gravel is enough. For badly broken drives, the fix may include removing mud-mixed material and rebuilding the base.

Why gravel driveways fail again

Almost every gravel driveway failure comes back to water. When rain sits on the surface or runs down the middle, it softens the base, and traffic pushes the stone into the mud. Potholes return in the same spots, and ruts reform.

The fix is shape and flow:

  • Crown. A slight high point in the center, or a consistent tilt to one side, so water sheds off the surface.
  • Edges. Shoulders that let water leave the drive instead of trapping it.
  • Where water goes next. Water shed off the drive needs a path away, such as a graded swale or a ditch line, so it does not pond at the edge and soften the base from the side.

Central Kentucky clay makes this especially important. Clay holds water and turns soft, so a drive built on clay without good drainage will keep breaking down no matter how much gravel goes on top.

What to flag before the machine shows up

A skid steer is heavy, and grading moves soil. Before the visit:

  • Utilities. Call 811 before any digging so buried lines can be marked. Point out anything you know about, such as water lines, electric runs to outbuildings, or invisible fence wire.
  • Septic. If you have a septic tank or drain field anywhere near the drive, mark it. Heavy equipment should stay off septic systems.
  • Gates and width. Measure gate openings and tight spots so the machine and any trailer can get in.
  • Soft ground. If part of the property is saturated, say so. Equipment can rut a wet yard, and some work is better done after a dry stretch.
  • Pipes under the drive. If there is an existing pipe or culvert under the driveway, point it out so it is not damaged.

Grading day vs a French drain trench

Both use machines, but they are different jobs.

Grading reshapes the surface. It moves soil and gravel to change how water runs across the ground. On a driveway, that means a crown and edges. In a yard, it can mean filling a low spot or shaping a gentle swale. Lawn Busterz does this with a skid steer.

A French drain moves water underground. It is a trench lined with filter fabric, filled with gravel around a perforated pipe, that collects water and carries it to an outlet. Lawn Busterz installs French drains and uses an excavator for trenching and drainage prep.

Some properties need both. A drive might be regraded to shed water, and a French drain or catch basin added where that water collects. See our guide on standing water in the yard for how to tell which fix fits.

What is outside this job

Skid steer grading for a gravel drive does not include paving, asphalt, concrete, culvert engineering, or septic work. Those need a different contractor. If your project requires an engineered culvert or a permit for work near a road ditch, check with your local road department or county before work begins.

Other skid steer work

The same machine handles more than driveways:

  • Leveling low or uneven areas of a yard before seed, sod, or a shed pad.
  • Gravel spreading for parking pads, paths, or work areas.
  • Demolition of small structures such as old sheds or decks.
  • Hauling soil, gravel, and debris on site.

What the finished drive should do

After grading, the next good rain is the test. A well-graded gravel drive should:

  • Shed water off the surface instead of pooling.
  • Show no new channels running down the center.
  • Hold firm under normal vehicle traffic.
  • Send water to the sides and away, not back toward the house or garage.

If water still pools after a regrade, that tells you where a drainage fix is needed.

Questions people ask.

Can a skid steer fix a rutted gravel driveway?

Yes. A skid steer can cut down ridges and high spots, pull gravel back from the edges, fill and compact ruts and potholes, rebuild a crown, and spread fresh stone. For many drives, regrading the existing material and adding a top layer of new gravel is enough. Badly broken drives may also need mud-mixed material removed and the base rebuilt.

Why do potholes keep coming back in my gravel driveway?

Water is almost always the cause. When rain sits on the drive or runs down the middle, it softens the base, and traffic pushes the stone into the mud. Without a crown or tilt to shed water, and a path for that water to leave, potholes return in the same spots. Central Kentucky clay makes soft spots worse.

What is a crown on a gravel driveway?

A crown is a slight high point running down the center of the drive, so water sheds to both sides instead of sitting on the surface. Some drives use a consistent tilt to one side instead. Either way, the goal is the same: move rainwater off the gravel quickly so the base stays firm and the surface holds up under traffic.

What should I do before a skid steer comes to my property?

Call 811 so buried utilities can be marked, and point out anything you know about, such as water lines or electric runs. Mark any septic tank or drain field so equipment stays off it. Measure gate openings, note soft or wet ground, and flag any pipe under the driveway. That lets the crew plan access and protect what is buried.

Is driveway grading the same as installing a French drain?

No. Grading reshapes the surface to change how water runs across the ground, such as a crown on a drive or a filled low spot. A French drain moves water underground through a gravel-filled trench with perforated pipe and filter fabric. Some properties need both.
